# Traintimes implementation plan

Centre the first display on **Hemel Hempstead (HML)** on the West Coast Main Line (WCML). Open there on first boot and offer a control to return to Hemel after panning or zooming. Users can still search for another Great Britain mainline station by name or three-letter code. Each sector follows connected running lines up to 15 rail miles from the selected station. [National Rail station record](https://www.nationalrail.co.uk/stations/hemel-hempstead/)

## Hemel Hempstead pilot sector

## Two decisions to make before buying hardware

**Prove the data around one station.** Network Rail's Train Describer (TD) feed reports a train description entering, moving between or leaving named berths. A berth is a logical position in the signalling system. Put the train label at the reported berth and move it on the next step. Signals have fixed locations and separate indications. Many mapped signals only report their most restrictive aspect versus a less restrictive one. Represent those as **on**, **off** and **unknown**. Display a specific colour aspect only where a documented indication distinguishes it. [Network Rail TD feed](https://raildata.org.uk/dashboard/dataProduct/P-8d5f90a7-2fce-4179-b233-0dd272cee896/overview) · [S-class messages](https://wiki.openraildata.com/index.php/S_Class_Messages) · [University of Birmingham on linking railway data](https://blog.bham.ac.uk/bcrre/2025/07/22/real-time-train-positioning-for-safety-performance-and-monitoring/)

**Choose where the live service runs.** Start on a laptop. For a single always-on appliance, the first hardware target is a **Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W and the 9-inch strip**. Precompute the map on the laptop. Test the live service and a lightweight native renderer together on the Pi. For an integrated touchscreen with an existing always-on host, the **Elecrow CrowPanel** is attractive. Run the same live service on the host and send just the selected sector to the panel over Wi-Fi.

| Decision | Pi Zero 2 W + 9-inch 1900 × 400 panel | Elecrow CrowPanel Advanced ESP32-P4 |
|---|---|---|
| Hardware to verify | Mini-HDMI output, 512 MB RAM. Confirm the strip has an HDMI controller, its native pixel mode, orientation, power and touch support. A bare DSI panel needs different hardware. The original Zero and Zero W have single-core CPUs. | The Amazon ASIN appears to match the 10.1-inch 1024 × 600 capacitive-touch model with 32 MB PSRAM and 16 MB flash. The UK listing could not be read directly. Confirm the selected variant and board revision against Elecrow's specification. |
| Display design | Put the main corridor left to right. Give connected branches their own labelled lanes. Test physical text size at the intended viewing distance. If the strip has no touch, use a phone on the same LAN for station search and zoom, or a USB keyboard. | Use the extra height for junctions, two-finger pan/zoom controls or dedicated touch buttons, and an on-screen station picker. Start with the vendor's ESP-IDF/LVGL display and touch examples. |
| Runtime | Raspberry Pi OS Lite, a small SDL renderer and a local feed service. Time map loading, memory use and reconnects on the actual Pi. An existing original Zero can be tested as a display client first. | ESP-IDF + LVGL. Download a compact vector map and state snapshot for the station. Cache the map, keep live state in RAM and redraw changed symbols. A standalone ESP32 version adds feed-client, credential and recovery work and is a later feasibility test. |

Hardware references are [Raspberry Pi Zero specifications](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/computers/zero.html) and [Elecrow's 10.1-inch CrowPanel guide](https://www.elecrow.com/wiki/CrowPanel_Advanced_10.1inch_ESP32-P4_HMI_AI_Display_1024x600_IPS.html).

## Build sequence

Estimates are hands-on time for one developer. The live-mapping estimate covers the initial stretch through Hemel. Estimate the rest of the 30-mile sector after measuring local signal-mapping effort and identifying the feed areas at the southern end.

### 1. Prove one corridor on the laptop · 1–2 days once access is available

- Start on **Apsley → Hemel Hempstead → Berkhamsted**. Use the Rugby **R1** train-describer area as the starting point and investigate **WJ** around Watford as the sector expands south. Use SMART entries and captured messages to establish the actual coverage and handovers. The area directory lists signal indications for both areas. The mapping from feed bits to individual signals at Hemel still needs verification. [Train-describer area directory](https://wiki.openraildata.com/index.php/List_of_Train_Describers) · [Network Rail SMART berth finder](https://vaildata.uk/bplan/berth-find)
- Request Network Rail's TD product on the Rail Data Marketplace. The listing describes a free beta subscription. The separate Network Rail open-feeds service is another access route and publishes its own account limit. Confirm receipt of both C-class berth messages and S-class signalling messages for the pilot. [Rail Data Marketplace TD subscription](https://raildata.org.uk/dashboard/dataProduct/P-8d5f90a7-2fce-4179-b233-0dd272cee896/overview) · [Network Rail open feeds](https://www.networkrail.co.uk/who-we-are/transparency-and-ethics/transparency/open-data-feeds/)
- Record a busy hour with source and receipt times. Replay one stopping train on a slow line and one through train on a fast line. Check direction, order and line assignment against the local diagram. Independently validate two local signal indications against a documented mapping and the observed changes. Use OpenTrainTimes' two adjacent diagrams as visual cross-checks. [Wembley Central to Tring](https://www.opentraintimes.com/maps/signalling/lec2) · [Tring to Hanslope Junction](https://www.opentraintimes.com/maps/signalling/r1_1)
- Release the verified train layer around Hemel even if signal decoding takes longer. Show known signal positions with unknown indications until their mappings are verified. Extend outwards on the WCML and measure coverage separately for each running line.

### 2. Generate a sector from a station name · 2–4 days

- Import rail station names, three-letter CRS codes and locations from NaPTAN. Index names and codes in SQLite. Offer locality and code when a name matches several stations. Associate each station with the running tracks it serves, including distinct platform groups at a junction. A station entrance coordinate alone can select the wrong track. [DfT NaPTAN](https://naptan.dft.gov.uk/) · [DfT NaPTAN schema guide](https://naptan.dft.gov.uk/naptan/schema/2.4/doc/NaPTANSchemaGuide-2.4-v0.57.pdf)
- Start with the current Network Rail Track Model from the Rail Data Marketplace. Use track geometry for lengths and validated junction connectivity for traversal. Check ambiguous junctions and station approaches against the local schematic. Preserve bridges and grade-separated crossings as distinct tracks. [Network Rail Track Model](https://raildata.org.uk/dashboard/dataProduct/P-d6c0c7ee-6743-4999-9b9e-d2dd39585bdb/overview) · [Description of track links, nodes and waymarks](https://github.com/openraildata/network-rail-gis)
- Define the default sector as the connected running-line continuations from the station, including branches at genuine junctions. Traverse in both directions from the station's track anchors with a 24,140.16 m distance budget. A branch reached after ten miles gets five more miles. Split the last segment at the limit, handle loops by shortest reachable distance and keep the cut ends labelled. Exclude depots and sidings from the overview.
- Save both rail geometry and schematic coordinates. For the overview, lay the main route along the wide axis, offset parallel tracks and branch lanes, and keep the order of stations, junctions and signals. Place labels once per zoom level. Zooming changes the level of detail within the same sector. Show stations and trains in the overview, then individual tracks, berths, signal IDs and facing directions in the detail view.
- **Pass when** a simple through station, a junction station and a grade-separated crossing give the expected routes and distance limits. Search must also open a static map for a station whose live coverage has yet to be mapped.

### 3. Attach live observations to the map · 3–5 days for the pilot

- Maintain two small, versioned lookups per area. One maps **(TD area, berth)** to a diagram position and track section. The other maps **(TD area, address, bit)** to a signal ID, its track position, facing direction and the meaning of each bit value. Record the source and revision for each mapping. Network Rail's SMART data anchors some steps to reporting locations. Check Hemel platform assignments against the sectional diagram and observed steps, and check the remaining berths against locally verified references. [Network Rail feed descriptions](https://www.networkrail.co.uk/who-we-are/transparency-and-ethics/transparency/open-data-feeds/) · [SMART berth finder](https://vaildata.uk/bplan/berth-find)
- For static signal positions, use the identified track and order on the schematic. Where geographic coordinates matter, match the signal reference and facing direction to a verified location. OpenStreetMap signal records are a possible source with their own attribution and licence. Keep a known signal visible with an unknown indication if its live mapping is missing. [UK railway signal mapping conventions](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/OpenRailwayMap/Tagging_in_the_United_Kingdom) · [OpenStreetMap attribution and licence](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright)
- Apply berth steps, cancellations and interpositions. Keep state per area and berth so reused train reporting numbers can appear independently. Identify mirror berths where neighbouring describers report the same movement. Apply S-class changes and refreshes according to the area's table. If passenger destinations or delay minutes are wanted later, add timetable or Darwin enrichment for confident matches. [TD message types](https://wiki.openraildata.com/index.php/TD) · [National Rail Darwin](https://www.nationalrail.co.uk/developers/darwin-data-feeds/)
- Emit one sector snapshot on selection, followed by sequenced changes over a WebSocket. After a display reconnect, send a fresh snapshot. After an upstream feed gap, mark affected observations stale and rebuild from verified replay or new observations and signal refreshes. An area heartbeat confirms connectivity. It cannot validate berth states missed during the gap.
- **Pass when** the same recording yields the same final state on every replay. A cancellation removes its label, an incoming train appears at the sector boundary, and an unplugged feed visibly enters a stale state. Record how many documented in-sector berths and signals have mappings and how many were observed in the sample. This distinguishes coverage from a quiet hour.

### 4. Put it on the selected device · 2–4 days

- Render the same busy recording at both native resolutions on the desktop first. Choose the display using the hardest view, such as several parallel tracks at a junction. Check whether train labels remain readable at normal distance and whether overlapping markers can be inspected by zooming.
- Build just the chosen device client. Add boot-to-map, last station recall and an always-visible feed status. On the ESP32, test touch and Wi-Fi together on the actual board revision. On the Pi, test the panel's HDMI mode after a cold boot and measure memory use with the collector running.
- **Accept the pilot** after a 24-hour run, a power cycle, a station change and a forced Wi-Fi outage. Target a 95th-percentile delay under two seconds from **local message receipt to redraw**, measuring upstream feed delay separately. Verify the map remains usable after the connection drops and that an area with missing coverage is recognisable at a glance.

### 5. Test expansion at a second station

Choose a junction whose 15-mile sector crosses a TD boundary. Generate the map with the same importer, add only the new area mappings and repeat the replay tests. Record the hours spent per area and publish coverage by station. That measurement gives a basis for deciding how much of Britain is practical to maintain.
