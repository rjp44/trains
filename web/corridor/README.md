# WCML train-describer panels

[Crewe to Birmingham International](https://transport.pickering.org/apps/crewe-birmingham/) covers 20 reporting locations via Stafford, Wolverhampton and Birmingham New Street. [Preston to Crewe](https://transport.pickering.org/apps/preston-crewe/) covers 15 via Wigan North Western and Warrington Bank Quay.

Read the route from left to right. The Up and Down running lines continue between reporting locations. Where the corridor has Fast and Slow lines, the diagram draws them separately and shows where they converge. The labelled station bands, short platform bars, signal indications and train descriptions use the Hemel panel conventions.

The running-line names stay on the left as you scroll. Trains sit on the rails. Empty track stays clear and mapped signals show their indications just above the line. The station register holds the underlying berth addresses and other signalling descriptions. The WCML panels and Hemel share the browser code that draws train descriptions and signal lamps, and use the same tablet renderer.

At major stations the diagram opens out into individual platform roads, through lines and bays. At Crewe the two unplatformed Fast lines run between platforms 5 and 6. Birmingham New Street includes the west-facing bay at 4C and the three centre sidings between platforms 5/6, 7/8 and 9/10. The shaded station throats summarise the connecting pointwork. Tapping the station name opens the complete register, including descriptions for which the source identifies the reporting location but leaves the physical track uncertain. [Crewe station schematic, page 11](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5aa25185e5274a3e3603a4e6/crewe-hub-consultation-response-print.pdf) · [Birmingham New Street station guide](https://www.networkrail.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Birmingham-New-Street-accessible-station-guide.pdf)

In **Settings → App → Train labels**, choose **Headcode** or **Operator + destination**. The latter pairs a coloured operator tag with the three-letter destination code. Tap a train for its headcode and service details. An unidentified service or destination keeps its headcode. The three signalling maps share this preference on the same tablet or browser.

Drag horizontally to follow the line. On the tablet, tap the bottom scroll bar or either Stop control to jump. In the browser use the Location selector, scroll bar or arrow keys. The station jumps centre the named location, including both endpoints. Tap a location name for **Watch** and its berth register, or a train for its service and running information when available. The same headcode can occupy adjacent berths during a movement.

Both panels show mapped signal indications at the Crewe end. The Birmingham panel also covers New Street and the line east to International. A filled red lamp means the most restrictive indication, an open green ring means a less restrictive indication, and a grey ring means the state is unknown. A berth without an identified signal address has no lamp. Tap a lamp for its signal number, address and reported state. A disconnected area keeps the last-known train positions and clears its indications.

On the tablet, the full route fits twice as much track across the screen as a sectional view. Spread two fingers over a station to open its detailed section. Pinch together to return to the full route at that station.

In the browser, **−** removes unused track space and **+** spreads it out. Station names, train badges and signals keep their size and shape. Zooming out stops when the labels, junctions and trains on screen need the remaining space. This limit adjusts as trains move. Click the percentage beside **Location** to reset. A two-finger pinch or Ctrl/⌘ + mouse wheel zooms around the point on the map. With the diagram focused, **−**, **+** and **0** also work. Each map remembers its own web zoom.

Settings → App selects the full route, either section or seven minutes of recorded movements. The tablet and browser read the same diagram and recording from the signed app package. The automatic theme follows Wolverhampton or Warrington.

The continuous railway is drawn from Network Rail’s route specifications and sectional diagrams. The named sections are crops of the same full-route drawing. The line through a station represents the selected corridor. Station reports alone do not identify every point, siding or route through a station throat. [Network Rail LNW route specifications](https://www.networkrail.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/LNW-Route-Specification.pdf) · [Network Rail sectional appendix, NW1001](https://filestore.limitofshunt.org.uk.s3.amazonaws.com/sectional-appendices/nr/lnw-n/module-1-2009.pdf)

SMART supplies the direction, line and platform for individual reporting steps. Connecting rail remains visible through areas without train reports. On the straight approaches at Leyland, Euxton and Madeley, a berth with no line code inherits its neighbour’s road only when the adjoining reported steps agree. [SMART field definitions](https://wiki.openraildata.com/index.php/Reference_Data#SMART:_Berth_Stepping_Data) · [Leyland source records](https://vaildata.uk/api/bplan/loc/LEYLAND)

The Dallam Junction Fast and Slow approaches, Crewe South Junction running lines and carriage sheds are identified in Network Rail’s London North Western (North) operating notice, printed pages 19–21. At Birmingham the pinned EASM diagram places train descriptions on the station approaches, centre sidings and the Aston branch at Stechford. Signal numbers and berth IDs can have different prefixes. A working indication requires a separately mapped signal bit. [Network Rail operating notice](https://sacuksprodnrdigital0001.blob.core.windows.net/sectional-appendix/Periodic%20Operating%20Notice%20and%20Sectional%20Appendix%20March%2025/London%20North%20Western%20%28North%29%20PON%20March%202025.pdf) · [EASM track plan](https://github.com/Shwam3/EASMData/blob/76ad5669ce8e351716a1b5b79e8c12fad60dd89b/images/maps/newstreet.png)

## Updating the maps

Run the corridor builder with Node.js to fetch SMART mappings from VailData and the pinned signal references. For an offline rebuild, pass a directory containing the TIPLOC JSON responses, `newstreet.json` and `CE.json`. The Crewe table identifies the berth for each signal bit. A route-set or platform latch bit cannot supply a signal aspect. Generated layouts and recordings live under each app’s data directory.
